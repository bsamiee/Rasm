Sound constructs serve a fact of the problem: a domain rule, an external contract, or an invariant. Ask of each module, type, function, and step which fact it serves. Constructs that serve another construct's shape, a value stored in the wrong place or form, a misplaced boundary, or a defect elsewhere are accidental complexity with an upstream cause. Duplicated derivations, definitions kept in sync by hand, conversions between representations of one fact, validation repeated after construction, and branches over states a type can exclude all point to that cause. Constraints the existing design assumes are decisions until the source that owns them states them as facts.

Trace the root cause back through every producer to the decision the construct serves and forward through every consumer. Rebuild the chain as a structural change in direct form: state each fact once in a single source of truth, derive every other form from it in the module that owns it, and encode invariants in types that make invalid states unrepresentable. In the same change, delete every construct that served the old shape and adjust each consumer to its absence. Rebuilds span every producer, consumer, module, and package the chain reaches, whatever the task named.

Local changes keep the defect and add coupling to it, whether they wrap, reimplement, rename, alias, or clean up the construct. Deprecations, shims, compatibility paths, partial fixes, and deferred notes keep the old shape. Rebuilds move only behavior and facts the problem needs into the new shape. Version control alone holds old code. Rebuilds produce less code, less indirection, and direct, current, optimized logic with one owner per fact.

Rebuilds are complete when the problem's facts alone explain every part of the structure, with no reference to its history.
