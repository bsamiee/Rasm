1. **Location** line 232, traversal policy table, row "Independent effects".
   **Claim** "instance `Traverse` under `IO` | parallel and unbounded, one thread per element, one failure fails all"
   **Evidence** `Seq<A>.Traverse` calls `Traversable.traverse`, and `Traversable<Seq>.Traverse` folds through
   `Applicative.lift`, so every step lands in `DSL.IOApply<A,B,C>.Invoke`, whose whole body is
   `ValueTask<Func<A,B>> ff = Ff.RunAsync(envIO); ValueTask<A> fa = Fa.RunAsync(envIO);` — no fork, no `Task.Run`,
   no thread anywhere. A synchronous `IO.lift` completes inside `RunAsync` before the next operand starts.
   Probe (`.scratch/lab-rev23`, three effects of 200 ms each): three `IO.lift` effects traversed with `Traverse`
   took **652 ms**, all on managed thread 1, in the order `1, end1, 2, end2, 3, end3`; the same three effects
   written with `IO.liftAsync` took **216 ms** under `Traverse` and **629 ms** under `TraverseM`.
   So `Traverse` overlaps asynchronous effects without a bound, runs synchronous effects in order on the calling
   thread, and forks no thread at all. The file's own `Traversals.Parallel` sample uses `IO.lift`, so the sample
   runs serially and contradicts its row.
   **Correction** replace the behavior cell with:
   `asynchronous effects overlap without a bound, one failure fails all`
   and add one sentence after line 237: `An effect lifted with `IO.lift` runs on the calling thread, so only
   `IO.liftAsync` effects overlap.` Rename `Parallel` in the doc and the lab (`Traversals.Parallel`,
   `Traversals.Bounded`'s argument) to `Overlapped` so the sample name matches the behavior.
