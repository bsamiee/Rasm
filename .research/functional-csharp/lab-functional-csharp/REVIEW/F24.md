# F24 review: 24-languageext-collections-and-shared-state.md

1. **`Seq<A>` is not strict, and `toSeq` does not always copy**
   - Location: line 9 (table row) and line 19 (prose).
   - Claim: "| `Seq<A>` | the default, strict, ordered |" and "`Seq<A>` is strict, so `toSeq` copies the source at once and a later change to the source has no effect. ... `Iterable<A>` defers every `Map` until `ToSeq` forces it."
   - Evidence: `Prelude.toSeq<A>(IEnumerable<A>)` (`DECOMP/Prelude.cs:5990`) copies only when the argument is a `Seq`, `Arr`, `A[]`, `IList<A>`, or `ICollection<A>`; every other `IEnumerable<A>` falls through to `new Seq<A>(value)`. That constructor is `public Seq(IEnumerable<A> ma) : this(new SeqLazy<A>(ma))` (`DECOMP/Seq_1.cs:343`), and the struct comment names the three representations `SeqStrict|SeqLazy|SeqEmptyInternal` (`Seq_1.cs:24`). `Seq<A>.Map`, the indexed `Map`, `Filter`, and `TakeWhile` all return `new Seq<B>(new SeqLazy<B>(Yield(...)))`, so `Seq` defers a `Map` exactly as `Iterable` does. The separating property is memoization: `Seq<A>` is documented "without the issues of multiple evaluation", while `Iterable<A>` is "a lightweight wrapper around `IEnumerable`" that re-runs its source. The file's own `Shapes.Doubling` hands `toSeq` a lazy `LanguageExt.List.unfold` enumerable, and LAB-NOTES (F17) records that this memoizes rather than copying. The lab probe passes a `List<int>`, which takes the copying overload, so it never exercised the general claim.
   - Correction: table row 9 becomes
     `| \`Seq<A>\` | the default, ordered, memoized | \`Seq(1, 2, 3)\`, \`toSeq(source)\` |`
     and the two prose sentences become
     "`toSeq` copies an array, a list, or a collection at once, so a later change to that source has no effect. `toSeq` over another `IEnumerable<A>` reads each item once and memoizes it. `Iterable<A>` holds no memory, so a second enumeration runs the source again."
