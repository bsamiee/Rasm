Before writing or rewriting prose in a file, load the `clean-prose` skill and follow it. Every reply follows the same rules.

TERMINOLOGY. Each word is the current established term of its language, tool, or field at the newest standard the context supports: it appears with that meaning in current documentation, names what a thing is instead of what it resembles, and names what an operation does instead of an image. Coined, metaphorical, and outdated terms, listed in the skill or not, take the current term for the thing they name, and a term that is real in its own field stays. The product name appears in identifiers the ecosystem requires and in prose only as a contrast with another product, and text under a heading that names its subject refers to the subject by pronoun or by its parts.

NAMES. Identifiers and every other name in code and build files say what the thing is in the vocabulary of their language, and a rename runs through the language tooling to update every reference. Names that other systems resolve stay unchanged as a coupling the report names. Prose writes code names in backticks with their exact spelling, shows a tool use as the command itself in place of a description of it, and keeps tool or product names used as words plain.

REMOVALS. Delete connectives, fillers and adverbs ending in -ly, marketing words, meta phrases, hedges with no uncertainty, and enumeration devices, then rebuild the sentence when the remainder does not stand on its own: a connective between clauses leaves a comma, and a connective between nouns becomes "with". "Such as" becomes a parenthetical or a list, "whose" becomes "with", "of", or "that", "due to" becomes "because", "e.g." and "i.e." become "for example" and "that is", and "etc." becomes the named items. Numbers stay as real values, frequency words stay when the source measured them and otherwise become the version or condition, and a hedge with real uncertainty stays as "can" or as its condition. Sentences that add nothing a nearby sentence lacks go, and any surviving fact moves into the nearest sentence as a few words.

SENTENCES. Each sentence states one complete thought in active voice and simple present or past, in the shortest form that keeps every fact. Facts about one subject join in one sentence with a comma or "and", a new subject opens a new sentence, and a sentence splits only at a change of thought. Em dashes appear only as `value — description` in a list item or table cell, and parentheses hold a phrase. Sentences, list items, and table cells open with the subject, pluralized or given "the" when the singular needs "a" or "an". Instructions use the imperative with the condition before the command and a comma between them, verbs name actions in place of nominalizations and phrasal verbs, and the actor replaces passive voice unless it is unknown. Modals are must, can, and will: "should" becomes "must" for a requirement and goes for a suggestion, the other modals become "can" or the condition, and "may have" before a participle stays. Articles and "that" stay, contractions expand, present perfect becomes simple past, "-ing" clauses after a comma fold in, one word names one concept for the whole text, and spelling is American.

DOCUMENTS. Each fact appears once, in the section with the heading that names its topic, in place of cross-references, citations, and sentences about the document itself. Headings are plain nouns without a parenthetical, and links survive only as the location of a thing the reader opens. Paragraphs hold one topic, parallel examples or cases sit in one sentence or one list in place of a run of short sentences, list items hold one sentence each in one grammatical form without a trailing period, and labels and sentence position give emphasis in place of bold and emoji. Table headers are one or two words, cells are values without a period, and rows are at most 150 columns.

COMMENTS. Comments state intent or a constraint the code cannot show, in one line and one statement without a trailing period, capitalized unless they start with a backticked identifier or a tool name. Each sentence stays whole on its line, consecutive full-line comments merge into one, and the comment that repeats the code goes. Section dividers, structured doc elements, commented-out configuration templates, and tool directives keep their form, and Python docstrings keep the first-line period. Log, error, and exception messages state what happened, then the cause when known, then the action, each in one sentence without a trailing period.

REPLIES. The reply opens with the answer or the outcome in complete sentences, with no opener, no closing offer, and no restatement of the request. Counts and measurements appear as digits in the sentence that reports them, in a table when the reply has more than three. Every file, function, or flag the reader must open appears with its path, and nothing else appears by name. Commands, snippets, and error text go in a fenced code block, lists hold parallel items, and prose holds a line of argument. After a rewrite, the reply reports bytes before and after, renames, couplings left in place, facts added or corrected, and facts kept in longer form because a shorter form loses them. Commit subjects are imperative, and commit and PR bodies state past facts.

FACTS. Every rewrite keeps every fact, adds no cause, frequency, or certainty the source did not state, and ends with fewer bytes unless a fact was wrong or missing. Text another system resolves or emits stays exact, and messages the code emits get the same rewrite.
